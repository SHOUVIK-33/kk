01 · The man

Singer Biography

Krishnakumar Kunnath, known to everyone as KK, was an Indian playback singer whose voice carried Hindi cinema's most romantic decade — and travelled south, east and west across seven languages.

He was born on 23 August 1968 in New Delhi to Malayali Nair parents, Kanakavalli Kunnath and C. S. Nair. The family name Kunnath came to him through matrilineal succession — a Malayali inheritance carried into a Delhi childhood.

He grew up in the capital, studied at Mount St Mary's School and then Kirori Mal College, Delhi University, where he took a commerce degree. For six months he was a marketing executive; between those months and the microphone there were years of singing in Delhi hotels to make ends meet.

The apprenticeship was extraordinary: around 3,500 advertising jingles before he ever sang for a film. That is where the tone was built — clean, warm, unhurried, able to carry a full orchestra or a single guitar.

His first film song was "Chhod Aaye Hum" in Vishal Bhardwaj's Maachis (1996), written by Gulzar. In 1999 came two arrivals that fixed his name: "Tadap Tadap" in Hum Dil De Chuke Sanam, and his debut album Pal with Lesle Lewis — the record that made "Yaaron" a farewell song for every Indian school.

He married Jyothy, his childhood sweetheart, in 1991. Their children are Nakul and Taamara. It was Jyothy, he often said, who pushed him to leave Delhi for Mumbai in 1994 — the move that made the career.

He died on 31 May 2022 in Kolkata, at 53, hours after performing at a college festival at Nazrul Mancha. He was cremated at the Versova cremation ground in Mumbai the next day, after the West Bengal government gave him a gun salute at Rabindra Sadan.

"As a singer, I haven't felt any lesser by not getting an award. Winning or not winning an award doesn't affect me. I just like to do my work in the best possible manner."KK, in a Hindustan Times interview — on nominations without wins

02 · Beginnings

Early Life & Childhood

A Delhi childhood with a Kerala name, a commerce degree, and three thousand jingles in between.

1968 · New Delhi

Born into a Malayali home

Son of Kanakavalli Kunnath and C. S. Nair, a Malayali Nair family living in Delhi. He grew up speaking Malayalam at home and Hindi in the street.

Inheritance

The name Kunnath

He received his family name through matrilineal succession — the tharavad line of Kerala, carried into a north Indian childhood.

School

Mount St Mary's

Schooled in Delhi, then Kirori Mal College, Delhi University, where he took a B.Com. Music was still a hobby, not a plan.

Six months

The marketing job

After graduating he worked as a marketing executive for six months. It was, by his own account, the shortest possible detour.

Hotel circuit

Singing for a living

He sang in Delhi hotels and clubs to make ends meet — hours of standards, ghazals and pop covers that taught him pitch, breath and audience control.

3,500

The jingle years

Before films he sang roughly 3,500 advertising jingles. He has said the discipline of a thirty-second spot is what made him a reliable studio singer.

07 · Recognition

Awards & Achievements

Two Screen Awards, eleven wins in all from thirty-six nominations — and a fan base that never needed a jury to decide.

The honours timeline

1999

Pal

His debut album with Lesle Lewis became one of the best-selling Indian pop albums of its time; "Yaaron" and "Pal" are still sung at every school farewell.

2000

Tadap Tadap

Ismail Darbar's composition for Hum Dil De Chuke Sanam brought him Filmfare, Screen and IIFA nominations — the song that made him a household name.

14 · The record

The Death Case — Investigation A to Z

On 31 May 2022 KK died in Kolkata, hours after a concert at Nazrul Mancha. This is the documented record — the medical findings, the police inquiry, the PILs and what was never established — updated to 9 November 2026.

Medical finding · SSKM Hospital autopsy

Cardiac death — no foul play

The post-mortem at SSKM Hospital, videographed and conducted in the presence of his family, cited myocardial infarction as the cause. The final report described death following hypoxia after acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, caused by a pathological cardiac condition, associated with subarachnoid haemorrhage. The chemical analysis report found nothing inconsistent with that.

What the doctors described: around 80% blockage in the left main coronary artery with smaller blockages elsewhere; a fatty layer around the heart that had turned white; stiffened valves; long-standing cardiac disease that had gone unaddressed. The autopsy surgeon said CPR given immediately after he lost consciousness might have saved him.

Police inquiry · New Market Police Station

Unnatural death case — no accused

On 1 June 2022 Kolkata Police registered a case of unnatural death — the standard procedure for a sudden death — and investigated the hotel room, the CCTV footage, the venue and the organisers. After the autopsy and chemical analysis, officers stated that the theory of an unnatural death could be ruled out.

No criminal case was filed against any person. Three public interest litigations asked the Calcutta High Court for a CBI (and in one, ED) probe into alleged mismanagement at the venue; the court sought the State's affidavit and remarks. No transfer of the investigation to a central agency has been confirmed in the public record.

Read this first. Nobody has been accused, charged or tried in connection with KK's death. The police inquiry ended without a criminal case, and his family did not file a complaint. What remains disputed is not the cause of death but the conditions at the venue and who was responsible for them — a question that produced PILs, political argument and new safety rules for college festivals.

    QuestionWhat the record saysStatus

    Status labels: Documented — stated in an official report or by police; Disputed — contested between parties; Unresolved — no public finding.

    Where the accounts collide

    The crowd Petitioners told the High Court that roughly 7,500 people were inside a hall with a stated capacity of about 2,400. The organising students' union said around 3,500 passes were printed, and the police commissioner said there was no shortage of space.

    The air conditioning KK was seen sweating heavily and asked for the spotlights to be switched off. Petitioners alleged the AC had failed under the crowd; the police commissioner said the AC was functioning properly.

    Medical cover Petitioners alleged there was no ambulance or doctor at the venue. The state pointed to the inquiry already under way and, after the death, Kolkata Police issued fresh conditions for college festivals — advance intimation, a doctor or ambulance on site, a pre-identified hospital, and passes limited to hall capacity.

    What is not established

    That anyone caused his death; that the crowd or the heat directly caused the cardiac event; or that a central agency took over the probe. The medical finding is a natural cardiac death; the negligence question was argued in PILs, not decided in a criminal trial.

    What happened next

    New rules for fests

    After the death, Kolkata Police required organisers to inform them in advance, keep a doctor or ambulance at the venue, identify a hospital in advance and issue passes within hall capacity.

    A statue, a doodle

    A statue was installed at Gurudas Mahavidyalaya in 2023, at the campus where he last performed. In October 2024 Google marked the anniversary of his playback debut with a doodle.

    The family

    Jyothy Krishna, Nakul and Taamara have kept his memory publicly — through paintings, posts and the music he left behind — while never filing a complaint about the death.
